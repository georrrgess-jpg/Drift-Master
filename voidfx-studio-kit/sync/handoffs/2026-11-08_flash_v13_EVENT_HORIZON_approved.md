## 3. Creative process
- **The song:** the full version's "drop" (from v8's screen recording) turned out to be a hats re-entry; the stems showed no drums in the section at all. The real structure is a 16-half-beat phrase with an 808 hit and a rest. So cuts land on the 808 hits and set pieces in the rests. Whisper on the full mix heard "girl, look out for me"; the vocal stem says "God".
- **The concept:** candidates were Savitar ("God, look out for me" = the god of speed; no footage), a Flashpoint "want vs need" reading, a wishing well / shooting star, and the Speed Force as a toxic lover. The S1 finale singularity won on footage (the S1 pack has it) and on physics (lensing, time dilation, tidal stretch, gravitational waves), which gave real-phenomenon transitions.
- **Rev 1's mistake:** with one fertile asset, every transition became a variation of the black hole: lens drop, photon ring, eye horizon, accretion whip, spaghettify, emblem horizon, and the loop fall. The physics were right and the variety was not.

## 4. Revision history
- **Rev 1, first delivery.** All automated checks passed; the review caught faces, seams, a lensed ghost of him, 30p judder and a flow warp before delivery.
- **Rev 2, the owner's note (verbatim):** *"To be honest with you, I really don't like this edit. I don't like that the singularity is the primary object in every transition and animation. I would like for you to make a emblem transition. I want you to do some more transitions that replace the previous singularity transitions. And I want you to remove some of the tedious sound effects. And I would like for you to just create a really cool graphic that you use to turn into a transition, and just try to smooth out the rest of the edit and polish it."*
  - **Diagnosis:** the hero asset was overused, so every cut looked the same. Rev 1's emblem transition was the black hole born in the emblem, so it didn't read as an emblem transition. 16 effects were still too many.
  - **Change:**
    - the singularity kept only in the "Darling," set piece;
    - ten new transitions, each a different idea;
    - the LIGHT RIBBON graphic (on screen as a graphic for ~1 s, then the transition, then the loop);
    - EMBLEM SPLIT;
    - sound cut to 11 events.
  - **Tried and failed on the way:**
    - the ribbon first orbited at chin level (across his mouth), and then read as a thin ring; it moved to the shoulders, with a second strand and a current;
    - the library `bolt_trace` sampled past its shot and showed the next pack shot (a park wide);
    - the afterimages were also drawn on Iris;
    - the emblem split's sliding halves pulled reflected edges in until the masks used a constant border;
    - the ribbon lingered across his face until it was faded once the loop opened.

## 5. What made it good / what made it weak
- **Good:**
  - the owner liked the rev 2 edit;
  - the studio's reading:
    - the variety of designed transitions in rev 2;
    - the emblem split (the emblem's own bolt becomes the cut);
    - a graphic that lives on screen before it turns into the transition (the ribbon);
    - the event horizon set piece timed to the song's 3 s vocal silence and deepest rest;
    - an exact loop (seam 0.12) that keeps moving across the wrap;
    - restraint in sound.
- **Weak:**
  - **The concept is abstract.** A black hole as the voice of a toxic love song needs explaining, and in rev 1 it took over the edit.
  - **Source quality:**
    - the edit is nearly all the S1 pack (1.1 Mb/s, detail 0.51), so it reads soft and grey under a desaturated grade;
    - the emblem shot measures SOFT (0.71× the median);
    - the newer bright-red suits (the run and the emblem) clash with the S1 maroon.
  - **Energy:** the section is drumless, so there are fewer beat moments; the first 10 s has no big moment before "Darling,".
  - **Faces:** the ribbon crosses his face for about 3 frames at its transition, and the loop's wipe crosses his face in the last half second.

## 7. New rules (adopt in both studios)
1. **The hero asset is a motif, not the transition kit.** Give it its set piece(s), and at most the focal piece and the loop: about 2–4 appearances. Every other transition is a different idea. This refines v12's "one hero asset carries the edit". Evidence: *"I don't like that the singularity is the primary object in every transition and animation"* (v13 rev 1 → rev 2). Applies to: all edits.
2. **An emblem transition is about the emblem itself.** Use its bolt or its shape, on the real tracked emblem: the bolt ignites, leaps off and splits the frame (v13 EMBLEM SPLIT), or the emblem cracks open (v12 EMBLEM BLOOM). Another effect that merely starts at the emblem doesn't count. Evidence: rev 1 had a black hole born in the emblem, and the owner still asked *"make a emblem transition"*. Applies to: Flash.
3. **A hero graphic earns its transition.** Let it live on screen as a graphic for about a second (its own motion, depth behind and in front of him), then let it become the transition, and keep it off faces. Evidence: *"create a really cool graphic that you use to turn into a transition"* → the LIGHT RIBBON, approved. Applies to: all.
4. **Start sound lean.** This is the third edit in a row where the owner cut effects (v12, v106, v13). Aim for about 10–12 sound events in 20–23 s. Small transitions are silent. Never use drones, swirls, whines or a swell on every move. Evidence: *"remove some of the tedious sound effects"* (rev 1, which already had about 16). Applies to: all.
5. **Check library transitions for source leaks.** A transition that samples its shot past the shot's range (v11 `bolt_trace`) can show the next shot in the pack. Pull its frames before using it. Evidence: v13 rev 2 review. Applies to: all.
6. **Technical:**
   - Fast 30p shots: raise the flow limit per shot (`flowmax`), and use a blend fallback (`flowfall="blend"`) where lightning jumps would warp.
   - When lensing or warping a background behind a matte, warp a clean plate (him inpainted out), or a ghost of him follows his edge.
   - QC measures length and the audio seam on the container duration; the decoded AAC carries padding.

   Evidence: v13 reviews. Applies to: all.
